## 1. Translating a visual direction into a usable space

I translated a teammate's theme planning and 2D designs into 3D spatial designs and renders for the exhibition and partner booths. I measured and dimensioned the designs to support the technical drawings produced by the builders, and advised on booth positioning, interaction affordances, spatial clearance, and circulation.

During installation, I referenced the renders and measured actual conditions to coordinate placement. When an additional two metres of clearance was requested on one side of the exhibition, I adapted the layout on site.

This work extended beyond producing a final image: the spatial proposal also became a reference for coordinating the build and responding to changes.

## 2. Giving each interaction a clear action and response

### Motion in Bloom — connecting movement to visible progress

Participants completed seated-to-standing-to-seated cycles to grow a garden. I contributed to ideation, created wireframes, and implemented a teammate's visual design using a largely AI-assisted workflow.

I refined the tracking behaviour around seated calibration, multi-frame confirmation, and separate rise and return thresholds. The system used head, shoulder, and hip anchor fallbacks, while central-player selection and masking outside the central portion of the image reduced bystander interference.

The feedback was tied to the movement: early upward motion began a flower animation, and the return to sitting completed the repetition. Completed blooms stayed visible until the next ascent. I also added camera setup/retry guidance, support for inverted webcams, and still-image fallbacks for failed video playback.

**Evidence to show:** A clip of a complete repetition with its flower response; an example of a tracking or camera-setup issue and the resulting refinement. The implementation changes do not establish real-person tracking accuracy or accessibility on their own.

### Protein Muncher — making educational content playable

I originated the game idea, planned its educational content, and created wireframes. A teammate developed the visual design, which I implemented through a largely AI-assisted workflow.

Players entered their weight and age group, then moved a catcher to collect foods toward a calculated protein target. I refined catching behaviour, fall speed, audio feedback, scaling, and image loading, and added four-language content and a larger result-screen QR code. The result provided a catch breakdown and food-reference guide.

**Evidence to show:** The input → gameplay → result sequence, an annotated wireframe/final comparison, and one concrete iteration. Confirm nutrition sources and client approval before publishing educational claims.

### CCD Thank You — connecting an individual contribution to a shared display

Visitors personalised a thank-you card and submitted it to a live wall. My work included prototype layouts and UX/system planning, followed by a rebuild after a teammate's UI redesign. I refined the editor, event navigation, and the immediate replay and spotlighting of a submitted card.

The deeper story is about the details between creating, submitting, seeing, and retrieving a contribution—not just the presence of a card editor.
